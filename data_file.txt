**What is the mode of operation feature for a joint demat account?**

CDSL has introduced a Mode of operation feature for joint demat account holders to enable any of the account holders to carry out specific operations without requiring the signatures of other account holders. Previously, the signatures of all account holders were necessary for operations in a joint demat account. However, with the introduction of the new mode of operation, any one of the joint account holders can independently perform such operations without requiring the signature of the other account holders.

The mode of operation is only applicable for the following operations:

*   **Off-market transfer:** Any account holder can transfer securities to other demat accounts without requiring the signatures of other account holders. 
*   **Pledge:** Any account holder can pledge securities with a bank or any NBFC without requiring the signatures of other account holders.
*   **Freeze/unfreeze:** Any account holder can freeze or unfreeze the securities in the demat account without requiring the signatures of other account holders.

To enable the mode of operations for the existing joint demat account with Zerodha, follow these steps:

1.  Print out the mode of operations form (PDF).
2.  Fill in the demat account number column with the 16-digit demat ID.
3.  Select the option Anyone of the holders or survivor(s) by ticking the appropriate box.
4.  Obtain the signatures of all joint account holders on the form.
5.  Courier the form to: Zerodha, 153/154, 4th Cross, J.P Nagar 4th Phase, Opp. Clarence Public School, Bengaluru - 560078

The mode of operations will be enabled within 72 hours of receiving the form.

To enable the mode of operations feature when opening a new joint demat account with Zerodha, tick the box next to Anyone of the holder or survivor(s) on page 16 of the joint demat application form. Alternatively, if someone decides not to enable the mode of operations, they can tick on Jointly.

**What is the Most Important Terms and Conditions page in the account opening forms?**

As per the NSE circular (PDF), starting April 1, 2024, new clients must sign the Most Important Terms and Conditions (MITC) (PDF) as part of the account opening forms. Existing clients must be informed about MITC by June 1, 2024.

The MITCs are summarized below:

* Keep the account safe by not letting anyone else trade on it without your permission. Do not share the login details with anyone.
* Funds or securities must be provided as collateral to Zerodha to trade or invest.
* Trading restrictions may be applicable as per Zerodha’s risk management policy.
* The tariff sheet (PDF) explains all the applicable charges.
* Purchased securities will be settled in the demat account by T+1 day.
* If a client purchases securities but fails to fully pay for them, a pledge with a specific time limit is created in favor of the stockbroker via the Clients Unpaid Securities Pledgee Account (CUSPA).
* Zerodha allocates client funds segment-wise with the Clearing Corporation (CC).
* Unutilized funds are transferred back to the client's primary bank account quarterly.
* A contract note is sent to the client within 24 hours of placing a trade.
* Clients can provide a Demat Debit and Pledge Instruction (DDPI) to Zerodha.

**What is a Depository Participant (DP) charge and how is it calculated?**

A Depository Participant (DP) charge is a fee levied by the depository (like CDSL) and the broker (Zerodha) for maintaining and transferring securities electronically.  It's applied when securities are sold from a demat account.

**DP Charge Calculation:**

* **Per day, per stock:**  If shares of the same company are sold multiple times in a day, the charge is applied only once. If shares of multiple companies are sold, the charge applies to each company's shares.
* **Gender of the primary demat account holder:**  Charges differ based on gender:
    * Male primary holder: ₹13 (₹9.50 Zerodha + ₹3.50 CDSL) + 18% GST = ₹15.34
    * Female primary holder: ₹12.75 (₹9.50 Zerodha + ₹3.25 CDSL) + 18% GST = ₹15.05
* **Mutual Funds:** No DP charges at Zerodha.

**Examples:**

**(Male Primary Holder):**

*   Selling the same stock multiple times in a day: ₹15.34
*   Selling different stocks on the same day: ₹30.68

**(Female Primary Holder):**

*   Selling the same stock multiple times in a day: ₹15.05
*   Selling different stocks on the same day: ₹30.09

DP charges appear on the funds statement, not the contract note.


**Why is the DP charge applied for Buy Today Sell Tomorrow (BTST) trades?**

DP charges are applied to BTST trades because of a change in the settlement process.  Previously, shares were credited and debited from the broker's pool account. Now, they are moved directly to and from the client's demat account, even for BTST trades.  This triggers the DP charge (₹13 + 18% GST for male primary account holders, ₹12.75 + 18% GST for female primary account holders) per day, per stock.  The change ensures corporate actions (bonuses, splits) and dividends are directly credited to the client's account and bank account, respectively.

**What is Securities Transaction Tax (STT) and how is it calculated?**

Securities Transaction Tax (STT) is a tax on buying and selling securities on Indian exchanges.  The STT rates were revised on October 1st, 2024 (see NSE circular PDF).  STT calculations are rounded to the nearest rupee (up if the paise amount is ≥50, down otherwise).

**STT Rates (New & Old):**

| Order Type        | New Charges                               | Old Charges                                   |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Equity Intraday   | 0.025% (₹25 per lakh) on sell side         | 0.025% (₹25 per lakh) on sell side             |
| Equity Delivery   | 0.1% (₹100 per lakh) on buy & sell sides   | 0.1% (₹100 per lakh) on buy & sell sides       |
| Options (Exercised)| 0.125% of intrinsic value (bought)         | 0.125% of intrinsic value (bought)             |
|                    | 0.1% of premium (shorted)                 | 0.0625% of premium (shorted)                 |
| Futures            | 0.02% (₹20 per lakh) on sell side         | 0.0125% (₹12.5 per lakh) on sell side          |


**Example Calculations:**

* **Equity Intraday:** 500 shares bought at ₹100, sold at ₹105. Average price = ₹102.5. STT = 500 * 102.5 * 0.025% = ₹13
* **Equity Delivery:** 500 shares bought at ₹100, sold at ₹105. STT (buy) = 500 * 100 * 0.1% = ₹50; STT (sell) = 500 * 105 * 0.1% = ₹53
* **Options (Exercised):** 1 lot (50 units) at ₹17,300 strike, spot ₹17,350. Intrinsic value = 50 * (17350-17300) = ₹2500. STT = 0.125% * ₹2500 = ₹3
* **Options (Premium):** 1 lot (50 units), ₹60 premium. STT = 0.1% * (60*50) = ₹3
* **Futures:** 1 lot at ₹750,000. STT = 0.02% * ₹750,000 = ₹150

**Will brokerage and taxes be considered when margins are blocked for trades in the commodity segment?**

Yes, an additional 0.01% of the turnover is blocked for MIS and CO commodity trades to cover CTT, exchange transaction charges, and SEBI charges.  This is added to the standard margin requirement.  For example, if the margin is ₹358,101 and the trade value is ₹621,400, an additional ₹62.14 will be blocked.  The blocked margin is released if the trade is closed at the same price.

**Why is GST being levied on SEBI charges?**

SEBI charges, levied on stock exchanges since January 1st, 2007, were previously exempt from GST. However, this exemption was removed, and an 18% GST is now applicable as of July 18th, 2022 (see SEBI circular PDF).

**What are clearing charges, and is there any at Zerodha?**

Clearing charges are fees paid to settle trades through a clearing agency.  This is a back-end process handling a broker's fund and security obligations with exchanges.

Zerodha, as a self-clearing member, does *not* charge its clients clearing fees. Other brokers who use third-party clearing members may charge ₹200 to ₹2,000 per crore (or 0.002% to 0.02% of the option premium), resulting in charges ranging from ₹30 to ₹300 on an intraday basis.  Clearing charges may be combined with other fees on some brokers' contract notes.



**What are call and trade charges?**

Zerodha levies a ₹50 + 18% GST call and trade charge for placing, modifying, or canceling orders via phone, or if Zerodha squares off positions due to margin shortfalls.

A separate ₹50 + 18% GST call and trade (auto square-off) charge applies when intraday positions (MIS and CO) remain open past the square-off time:

*   Equity: 3:20 PM
*   Equity F&O: 3:25 PM
*   CDS: 4:45 PM
*   MCX: 25 minutes before market close

(These times are subject to change; check zerodha.com/marketintel/bulletin.)

For auto square-offs, a maximum of 30,000 quantities per order are closed to limit price volatility.  Larger positions are squared off in multiple orders, incurring multiple charges (e.g., 100,000 quantities would result in four orders and a ₹200 + 18% GST charge).  For F&O, the maximum quantity per order is the quantity freeze limit (available from the NSE website - WEB link).

**What is the difference between an NRE and NRO bank account?**

NRIs in India cannot use regular savings accounts and must use either an NRE or NRO account.

| Basis             | NRE Bank Account                                      | NRO Bank Account                                   |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Uses              | Park/transfer foreign earnings (fully repatriable)      | Manage income earned in India                       |
| Repatriability    | Full repatriation of principal and interest             | Full interest repatriation; principal repatriation limited² |
| Joint Account      | All holders must be NRIs                               | Can include Indian citizens or other NRIs          |
| Deposits/Withdrawals | Foreign currency deposits only; INR withdrawals only    | Deposits in foreign or INR currency; INR withdrawals |
| Taxability        | Interest is tax-free                                   | Interest taxed at 30.9% (30% + cess & surcharge) |
| Inter-transfer     | Funds cannot be transferred from NRO to NRE             | Funds can be transferred from NRE to NRO           |

¹NRE accounts allow full repatriation (principal and interest).
²NRO accounts have limited repatriation (up to $1 million annually).  For F&O trading, link an NRO account.  NRE accounts are used for NRE PIS accounts; NRO accounts are used for NRO Non-PIS accounts.

**How are funds in NRI accounts settled after the sale of securities?**

**PIS Account:**

Funds are settled to the linked bank account on T+1 and available for trading from T+2 due to RBI reporting and bank TDS computation.

**Example:** Stocks sold Monday (T-day); contract note sent to bank Monday; funds credited Tuesday (T+1); bank report sent to Zerodha Tuesday; funds available as margin Wednesday (T+2).

**Non-PIS Account:**

75% of proceeds are available for trading on T-day.  The remaining 25% is released on T+1 after provisional TDS for capital gains is blocked and reversed. Funds are available for trading from T+2.

**Example:** ₹20,000 of stocks sold Monday (T-day); ₹15,000 released Monday; ₹3,588 provisional TDS blocked; provisional TDS reversed Tuesday; full amount available for withdrawal Tuesday, trading from Wednesday (T+2).  Actual TDS is deducted if there's a profit; no TDS if there's a loss.  Note that Orbis manages trade settlement and margin reporting for NRO F&O account holders.  See "What are the trading restrictions for NRIs?" for more details.

* **Mutual Fund Investment:** Prohibited for US and Canadian NRIs. Other NRIs can invest if they have an NRI Zerodha account. (See "What documents are required to open a Zerodha account as an NRI?")
* **Intraday Equity Trading:** Not allowed.
* **BTST Trading:** Not allowed.
* **Sovereign Gold Bond (SGB) Purchases:** Not allowed; existing holdings can be sold or held.
* **Intraday F&O Trading:** Allowed only with a CP code.
* **Currency and Commodity Trading:** Not allowed.
* **Account Linking and Trading:** With a linked NRE account, only equity trading is allowed.  With a linked NRO account, either equity delivery or F&O trading is permitted.
* **Pledging Securities:** Pledging securities as collateral for F&O trading is not permitted.

**What is the trading process for NRI accounts?**

The NRI trading process differs based on account type:

**PIS Accounts:**

*   **Adding Funds:** Funds are transferred from the NRE or NRO bank account to the PIS bank account; the bank sends a balance report to Zerodha at day's end; the PIS balance is updated as margin in the Zerodha account the next trading day. (See "What are the market timings?")
*   **Buy Orders:** Zerodha sends a buy contract note to the bank at day's end; the bank debits the PIS account and credits Zerodha on T+1.
*   **Sell Orders:** Zerodha sends a sell contract note to the bank at day's end for TDS calculation; the PIS account is credited on T+1.

**NRO Non-PIS Accounts:**

*   **Adding Funds:** Funds are transferred directly via payment gateway, NEFT, RTGS, or IMPS. (See "What are the different ways of transferring funds to my Zerodha account?")
*   **Trading:** Similar to resident accounts; an NRO bank account is linked.

**Futures & Options (F&O) Trading:**

NRI F&O trading is through an NRO account and requires a CP code from a custodian (Orbis is a Zerodha partner; see orbisfinancial.in/).  Funds are held by the custodian. (See "How can I open a Zerodha trading and demat account as an NRI?")

**Why is there a mismatch between the PIS account balance and what is updated on Kite?**

Zerodha's risk management deducts the following from the PIS closing balance before updating Kite:

*   0.5% of the PIS closing balance.
*   Bank reporting charges of ₹118 (₹100 + 18% GST).
*   Value of shares bought on that day.

These deductions ensure sufficient funds in the PIS account to prevent settlement delays.

**Examples:**

*   **Scenario 1 (No trades):** ₹10,00,000 PIS balance; Kite balance = ₹10,00,000 - (₹5,000 + ₹118) = ₹9,94,882
*   **Scenario 2 (Trades):** ₹15,00,000 PIS balance, ₹9,94,000 worth of trades; Kite balance = ₹15,00,000 - (₹7,500 + ₹118) - ₹9,94,000 = ₹4,98,382

**How to understand the funds statement for an NRI Zerodha account?**

NRI trades are settled on a bill-to-bill basis, based on contract notes sent to the bank.  Ledger entries appear on the transaction day; bank entries appear per the settlement cycle. (See "What is the trading process for NRI accounts?")

*   **Buy Transactions:** A debit entry shows a buy transaction (e.g., ₹40,434 debit on Oct 29th, 2020, with a corresponding bank entry on Oct 31st, 2020).  The PIS statement will also show this debit per the settlement cycle.
*   **Sell Transactions:** A credit entry shows a sell transaction (e.g., ₹48,478.82 credit on May 3rd, 2020, with a corresponding bank entry). The PIS statement will show this credit per the settlement cycle.

Note that some banks may not consider IPOs, ETFs, and bonds as part of PIS regulations and settle them in savings accounts.



**How does Zerodha deduct TDS for NRI NON-PIS accounts?**

Zerodha deducts Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) for NRI Non-PIS accounts.  Capital gains are categorized as short-term (STCG) or long-term (LTCG).

**Tax Rates:**

| Segment    | Capital Gains | Period             | Rate | Surcharge | Cess            |
| ---------- | -------------- | ------------------ | ---- | --------- | --------------- |
| Equity     | STCG           | < 365 days          | 20%  | 15%       | 4% + surcharge   |
|            | LTCG           | > 365 days          | 12.5% | 15%       | 4% + surcharge   |
| Non-Equity | STCG           | < 3 years           | 30%  | 15%       | 4% + surcharge   |
|            | LTCG           | > 3 years           | 20%  | 15%       | 4% + surcharge   |

**STCG TDS:** For stocks held <365 days, a provisional 20% + cess TDS is applied at day's end; this is reversed on T+1, and the actual TDS (23.92% on profits) is deducted.  No TDS is deducted on losses.

**LTCG TDS:**  No LTCG tax below ₹1,25,000.  Above ₹1,25,000, a 14.95% LTCG tax (12.5% + 15% surcharge + 4% cess) is applied for shares held >365 days (the grandfathered amount is not considered).  If no LTCG is accrued, you can claim TDS when filing taxes.

**Capital Gains Statement:**  Emailed on T+1; includes summaries of daily P&L, TDS computation (with adjusted gains after considering carry-forward losses), and carry-forward losses (opening balance, additions/deductions, and closing balance).  STCL is set off against STCG and LTCG; LTCL is set off against LTCG only.  Update buy prices for transferred shares before selling to avoid incorrect TDS deductions.  Losses are carried forward within the financial year.  Note that from July 1st, 2023, the ITD has deactivated PANs not linked to Aadhaar, impacting NRIs without updated residential status (see "How to update the resident status of PAN for an NRI & PIO account?" and WEB link).



**What is the “Provisional TDS amount blocked” entry in the ledger?**

For NRI Non-PIS accounts, Zerodha deducts TDS.  A provisional TDS entry (20% + cess on sale proceeds) is created in the ledger at day's end. On T+1, this is reversed, and the actual TDS (based on capital gains) is deducted if there's a profit; no TDS is deducted on losses.  (See "How to interpret the ledger statement for an NRI Zerodha account?")

**Example:**  ₹15,844.77 sale; ₹3,790.07 (approx. 23.92%) provisional TDS blocked at day's end; reversed on T+1; actual TDS is debited on T+1 if there's a profit; losses are carried forward.  For LTCG, a 12.5% tax applies to profits above ₹1,25,000 (no grandfathered amount considered).  If no LTCG, TDS can be claimed when filing taxes.  Update buy prices for transferred shares before selling to avoid incorrect TDS.  (See "How does Zerodha deduct TDS for NRI NON-PIS accounts?")  Note that the ITD deactivated PANs not linked to Aadhaar from July 1st, 2023, affecting NRIs without updated status (see "How to update the resident status of PAN for an NRI & PIO account?" and WEB link).

**How are Kite app code and external TOTP better than SMS OTP?**

While SMS OTP is common, it has drawbacks in capital markets:

*   High SMS volume during market hours can lead to delivery delays or failures.
*   Daily logouts necessitate frequent logins, increasing vulnerability to SMS delays/failures.
*   Reliance on telecoms creates systemic risk for timely authentication.
*   SMS is insecure, unencrypted, and non-cryptographic; messages can be intercepted.
*   SIM hijacking is possible through social engineering.

Kite app code and TOTP offer advantages:

*   Cryptographically secure (only the recipient can see the code).
*   Short validity (30 seconds), with automatic code regeneration.
*   Additional authentication layer (biometrics) and hardware-based storage/generation.
*   No external network connectivity needed.

(See "How to set up Time-based OTP (TOTP) to log in to Kite?" for TOTP setup instructions.)

How to close the Zerodha account? -  You cannot close your account if you have a negative balance. All positions must be squared off, and holdings must be sold or transferred before closure. You can choose closure-cum-transfer (see Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for closure cum transfer (PDF) and CDSL operating instructions for depository participants (PDF)). Download necessary reports from console.zerodha.com before closing, as they won't be accessible afterward. You cannot reopen the same account with the same User ID.  To open a new account, see "How to open a Zerodha account online?". Multiple account closures with the same PAN may lead to application rejection.

**Online Closure (Resident Indians, NRIs, minors with Kite access):** Log in to console.zerodha.com, click Account, then Segments, then Close account. Select a reason, provide feedback, click Continue, accept terms, click Proceed to eSign, then Sign now. Authorize NSDL, enter Aadhaar number, and verify the OTP.

**Offline Closure (Joint accounts, minors without Kite access, commodity account only closure):** Create a ticket with the e-signed account closure form.  Joint account holders and minors require all holders'/guardian's signatures. See "How to eSign documents?".

**Offline Closure (Non-individual accounts):** Complete the account closure form and courier it to Zerodha's Bengaluru address.  Authorized signatories (as per board resolution or authority letter) must sign for corporate, OPC, LLP, partnership, trust, AOP, and HUF accounts.

Account closure takes 3 working days; a confirmation email will be sent.  The account can be temporarily deactivated using Kill Switch.  If closing due to employer restrictions, see "How can Zerodha be empanelled as a registered broker with a company?".  Closure may be rejected if holdings have undergone corporate actions (bonuses, splits).
